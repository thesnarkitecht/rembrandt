# Privacy: the Rembrandt apps

This covers the Rembrandt apps for iPhone, iPad, Android, Mac, Windows and Linux, and copies of the
web editor you run yourself. Cloud sync, when it opens, has its own privacy policy.

**Rembrandt doesn't collect any data about you.** There's no account, no analytics, no advertising
and no tracking.

- **Your photos stay on your device.** Rembrandt opens, edits and saves them on the device. They
  aren't uploaded anywhere unless you share or export them yourself.
- **AI features run on your device.** Subject selection, depth, Lens Blur and Refocus run locally.
  The AI library Rembrandt uses (Google's MediaPipe) normally sends usage statistics to Google;
  Rembrandt blocks those requests, so nothing is sent.
- **Purchases.** The phone app's one-time unlock is bought through the App Store or Google Play.
  Apple or Google handle the payment; Rembrandt never sees your payment details, name or email. The
  app asks the store whether this account owns the unlock.
- **Update check.** The apps check whether a newer version exists: the desktop apps ask GitHub, where
  Rembrandt is published, and the phone app asks the Rembrandt website. Like any web request, this
  shows your IP address to that server. Nothing about you or your photos is sent.
- **Services you connect.** If you import from or export to Google Photos, Google Drive, Dropbox,
  OneDrive, iCloud or Adobe Lightroom, the app talks to that service directly, using your sign-in
  with it. Rembrandt's makers never receive those photos or your sign-in. Each service's own privacy
  policy applies.
- **Permissions.** The phone app asks for access to your photo library only to open the photos you
  choose and to save your edits there.

Rembrandt doesn't knowingly collect anything from anyone, including children.

Questions: open an issue at https://github.com/thesnarkitecht/rembrandt/issues.

Changes to this policy are published in this file, with its history in the repository.
